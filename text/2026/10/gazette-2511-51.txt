Every Financial Institution shall take measures specified in these rules for the purpose of identifying, assessing and understanding its money laundering, terrorist financing and financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction risks (hereinafter referred to as the “risk”) associated with its customers, the countries or regions in which those customers are engaged in business, its products, services, transactions and delivery channels by conducting customer due diligence (hereinafter referred to as the “CDD”) and apply a risk-based approach for allocating resources, monitoring, managing and mitigating such risk. 4. The intensity and extensiveness of risk management shall be commensurate with the level of risk and be proportionate to the nature, scale and complexity of the Financial Institution’s activities and its risk profile by applying a risk-based approach. 5. (1) Every Financial Institution shall conduct appropriate processes, including the following, in identifying, assessing and understanding its risk:- (a) obtain approval of the Board of Directors for the risk appetite and the risk tolerance level of the Financial Institution; (b) carry out institutional risk assessment to determine the level of risk and the appropriate types of mitigation to be applied in relation to the level of risk by considering all relevant risk factors including but not limited to the national risk assessment on money laundering, terrorist financing and financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction; (c) document such risk assessment including findings and action plans, to manage the risk, with timelines; (d) keep the risk assessment up-to-date through a review, at least annually and more frequently as needed; (e) advise the Board of Directors of such risk assessment and the measures to manage the risk; (f) obtain the approval of the Board of Directors for such risk assessment and any update thereto; and (g) provide risk assessment information to the relevant supervisory authority or the Financial Intelligence Unit as the case may be. (2) It shall be the duty of the Board of Directors to provide sufficient resources required to conduct the processes specified in sub rule (1). 6. Internal Controls, Policies, Procedures, Compliance, Audit and Training 12. (1) Every Financial Institution shall formulate an internal policy approved by its Board of Directors subject to the written laws in force for the time being, on anti-money laundering, countering the financing of terrorism and financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction. Such policy shall be reviewed annually or sooner by the Financial Institution, whenever there are changes to the risk or such written laws, and the approval for such reviewed policy shall be obtained from its Board of Directors. (2) The detailed procedures, controls and systems shall be developed by each Financial Institution in compliance with such policy and be approved by the senior management. (3) Such policy, procedures, controls and systems shall include but not limited to, risk assessment procedures, CDD measures, requirement of record retention, handling of wire transfers and attempted transactions, reliance on third parties, compliance with targeted financial sanctions, anti-money laundering, countering the financing of terrorism and financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction audits, the detection and internal reporting procedure of unusual or suspicious transactions, the obligation to report suspicious transactions in terms of section 7 and transactions specified under section 6 of the Act to the Financial Intelligence Unit, transaction monitoring, handling of breaches, escalation mechanism, whistleblowing, testing, new product, service, delivery channel or technology approvals, avoidance of tipping-off and confidentially obligations and termination of business relationship. 13. Every Financial Institution shall in formulating policies, procedures, systems, and controls take into consideration the risk that may arise from the use of new or developing technologies, including those having features of anonymity and inconsistency with the CDD measures specified in Part II of these rules. 14. Every Financial Institution shall - (a) provide the compliance officer appointed in terms of the provisions of the Act, with sufficient resources to ensure the duties, functions and obligations of the Financial Institution under the Act and these rules are performed and discharged; (b) ensure that such compliance officer or any other person authorized to assist the compliance officer or act on behalf of the compliance officer, has prompt access to all customer records and other relevant information, systems and personnel which may be required to discharge the compliance officer’s functions; (c) develop and implement a comprehensive employee and agent due diligence and screening procedure to be carried out at the time of appointing, entering into a contract or any agreement or hiring of employees whether permanent, contractual or outsourced or agents; (d) design and implement, on-going, periodic training and awareness programmes for employees including the Board of Directors, in order to effectively implement the regulatory requirements and internal policies and procedures relating to risk management. The training and awareness programmes shall include, but not limited to, understanding the relevant written laws, institutional overall risk, new developments in money laundering, terrorist financing and financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction techniques, methods and trends and their responsibilities relating to the combating of the risk, requirements relating to CDD, targeted financial sanctions and identifying suspicious or unusual transactions and reporting obligations including suspicious transactions; and (e) maintain an independent audit function that is adequately resourced and able to regularly assess the effectiveness of the Financial Institution’s implementation of internal policies, procedures controls and systems and, its compliance with the Act, rules, regulations, directions, circulars and guidelines made or issued under the Act. (1) In terms of the provisions of subsection (3) section 2 of the Act, a Financial Institution shall not open, operate or maintain an account, where the holder of such account cannot be identified, including any anonymous account or any account identified by number only, or any account which to the knowledge of the Financial Institution is being operated under a fictitious or false name (hereinafter referred to as “Fictitious or Anonymous Account”). (2) A Fictitious or Anonymous Account includes an account where the ownership is transferrable without the knowledge of the Financial Institution and accounts that are operated and maintained with the account holder’s name omitted. 19. (1) Every Financial Institution shall maintain information in such a manner that the accounts, records and information in relation to assets and liabilities of a given customer of such Financial Institution can be readily retrievable. (2) A Financial Institution shall not maintain accounts separate from the Financial Institution’s standard operational processes, controls, systems or procedures. 20. Where a Financial Institution is unable to comply with CDD measures specified in these rules, such Financial Institution shall – (a) in relation to a new customer, not open the account or commence the business relationship or perform the transaction; or (b) in relation to a customer who has already established a business relationship, terminate such business relationship, and consider making a suspicious transaction report in relation to such customer. 23. Where a Financial Institution suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect the commission of an offence of money laundering, terrorist financing or financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction or any unlawful activity and if there are reasons to believe that conducting CDD measures will tip off the customer, such Financial Institution is permitted not to conduct CDD measures and shall immediately make a suspicious transaction report. Risk Based Due Diligence 24. Without prejudice to the provisions of rule 21, where any customer is rated as a customer posing a low risk, the Financial Institution may conduct simplified CDD measures specified in Schedule II hereto, as the Financial Institution may deem appropriate, but not limited to, the measures specified in Schedule II. 25. (1) Where any customer is rated as a customer posing a high risk, the Financial Institution shall conduct enhanced CDD measures for such customer, as specified in Schedule III hereto, in addition to the CDD measures specified in rule 21 and any other measures as the Financial Institution may deem necessary. (2) Every Financial Institution shall, in conducting the enhanced CDD measures specified in Schedule III, determine the appropriate measures applicable to each and every customer. Customer and Transaction Monitoring 29. Every Financial Institution shall monitor all business relationships on an ongoing basis to ensure that the transactions are consistent with the information obtained under these rules, the risk profile of such customer and the Financial Institution’s knowledge of such customer. 30. (1) Every Financial Institution shall obtain information and examine the background and purpose of all complex, unusually large transactions and all unusual patterns of transactions, which have no apparent economic or prima facie lawful purpose. (2) The background and purpose of such transactions shall be inquired into and findings shall be kept on record with a view to making such information available to the relevant competent authority, supervisory authority or the Financial Intelligence Unit when required to do so. 31. Every Financial Institution shall increase the number and timing of controls applied and select patterns of transactions that need further examination, in relation to high risk business relationships. 32. Every Financial Institution shall have an effective internal mechanism to report any suspicious transaction to the Financial Institution’s compliance officer for appropriate action, in compliance with the Act and these rules. 33. (1) Every Financial Institution shall periodically– (a) review all customer relationships. Such reviews shall include assessing the adequacy, accuracy and up-to-dateness of customer information held by the Financial Institution, changes in customer profile such as beneficial ownership, customer behaviour, delivery channels, products used, source of funds, client’s account activity and verification of customers against any regulation made in terms of the United Nations Act, identification of politically exposed persons and adverse media screening; and (b) re-assess and re-classify the customer’s risk rating, as needed. (2) The review period and procedures thereof shall be decided by each Financial Institution on a risk-based approach as laid down in its internal policy for anti-money laundering, countering the financing of terrorism and financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction: Provided however, for high risk customers a periodic review shall be conducted at least once during a period of one year. Existing Customers 35. Every Financial Institution shall conduct CDD measures as may be appropriate on its existing customers having regard to its own assessment of materiality and the risk but without compromising the identity and verification requirements. In assessing the materiality and risk of an existing customer, a Financial Institution may consider the following:- (a) the nature and circumstances surrounding the transaction including the significance of the transaction; (b) any material change in the way the account or business relationship is operated; or (c) the insufficiency of information held on the customer or change in customer’s information. 36. Every Financial Institution shall conduct CDD measures on existing customer relationships at appropriate times, considering whether CDD measures have previously been conducted, the adequacy of documents, information, or data obtained, and ensuring that documents, information, or data collected through CDD measures are kept up-to-date and relevant through regular reviews of existing records. Third Party Customers 37. Every Financial Institution shall with regard to all transactions including cash deposits exceeding Sri Lanka rupees two hundred thousand or its equivalent in any foreign currency deposited into an account separately or in aggregate by a third party customer, have on record the name, address, identification number of a valid identification document, purpose and the signature, where applicable, of the third party customer: Provided however, clerks, accountants, employees, agents, or authorized persons of business places who are authorized to deal with the accounts shall not be considered as a third party customer. Non-Governmental Organizations, Not-for-Profit Organizations or charitable institutions 41. Every Financial Institution shall, in addition to the other provisions of these rules, conduct CDD measures when entering into a business relationship with a Non-Governmental Organization (hereinafter referred to as “NGO”), a Not-for- Profit Organization (hereinafter referred to as “NPO”) or charitable institution to ensure that their accounts are used for and the transactions are commensurate with the declared objectives and purposes of such NGO, NPO or charitable institution as per their constituent documents. 42. (1) Every Financial Institution shall open accounts in the name of the relevant NGO, NPO or charitable institution as per the title given in their constituent documents. (2) The persons who are authorized to operate the accounts on behalf of any NGO, NPO or charitable institution and the members of the governing body of any NGO, NPO or charitable institution shall also be subject to CDD measures. 43. A Financial Institution shall not allow personal accounts of the members of the governing bodies of a NGO, NPO or charitable institution to be used for the objectives and purposes of such NGO, NPO or charitable institution. 44. Every Financial Institution shall review all existing relationships and monitor all transactions of a NGO, NPO or charitable institution to ensure that those organizations and institutions, their authorized signatories, members of their governing bodies and the beneficial owners are not linked with any designated person, group or entity as referred to in rule 99.
